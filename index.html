Project 01 — Research paper

Can a mushroom replace a plasterboard?

An experimental comparison of mycelium-based composites and the standard BA13 gypsum panel: thermal insulation, fire and water.

Plasterboard is the default interior wall: cheap, easy to fit, and naturally non-combustible. It is also made of quarried gypsum calcined at high temperature. Mycelium, the root network of fungi, can instead digest agricultural waste and bind it into a light, biodegradable solid.

Two formulations were grown for the study: Pleurotus ostreatus on wood chips and Ganoderma lucidum on grain. Colonised substrate was crumbled, packed into custom 3D-printed moulds, incubated for a week, unmoulded to grow a protective fungal “skin”, then dried for about six hours at 60 °C to stop growth.

Verdict. Raw mycelium cannot yet replace BA13 everywhere — Euroclass fire ratings remain out of reach, and one wood-chip sample kept burning. But the grain formulation insulated better and resisted water better than plasterboard. Caveat noted in the paper: the mycelium panels were 18 mm thick against 12.5 mm for BA13.

Project 02 — Internship, Paris · Campus Cyber

Reading the weather by radio

Mounting, pairing and maintaining a multi-sensor Ecowitt weather station — then intercepting its own radio frames with a USB dongle.

One of the first assignments of my internship at Campus Cyber, in La Défense: the full physical installation and network configuration of a weather station, from mast alignment to firmware updates.

The second half was the eye-opener. With an RTL-SDR key — a software-defined radio receiver — we captured, visualised and decoded the raw frames the sensors were broadcasting. Invisible data streams are everywhere, and they can be read with the right fundamentals and open-source tools.

  • GW1100Wi-Fi gateway. Collects every sensor's RF signal; measures indoor temperature, humidity and pressure.
  • WS90 “WittBoy”Seven-in-one array: ultrasonic anemometer, haptic rain gauge, temperature, humidity, UV, light. Solar powered.
  • WH57Lightning detector with a range of up to 40 km — kept well away from motors and servers to avoid false positives.

Project 03 — Internship, French Guiana · Guiana Space Centre

Snake Eye

A mobile, passive forensic radio box designed to map every wireless network around a launch site.

If a launch goes wrong, a spaceport has to be able to show that the anomaly was not caused by an outside action on its radio environment. Snake Eye is the collection kit imagined for that job: one Linux “brain”, three radio modules, no soldering.

The starting point was an audit question: could someone within radio range listen to the wireless intercoms used by ground crews? From there the scope widened to three domains — Bluetooth LE, private Wi-Fi and LoRaWAN — captured side by side and merged into time-stamped .pcap files.

Mode A

Black box

Boots, launches tshark on every radio interface and records to the SD card, fully offline. Analysis happens later, in the lab.

Mode B

Live stream

One SSH command pipes all three captures in real time into Wireshark on the analyst's laptop.

Raspberry Pi OS Lite · rtl8814au (DKMS) · airmon-ng · nRF Sniffer · Python 3 · tshark · OpenSSH

  • Raspberry Pi 5The brain — powers, time-stamps, merges
  • Alfa AWUS1900Wi-Fi 2.4 / 5 GHz, monitor mode
  • nRF52840Bluetooth LE sniffer, follows channel hops
  • RTL-SDR v4LoRaWAN / IoT, 868 MHz antenna

Project 04 — Product design · Kickstarter

CloudCaps

A design-led bottle opener sculpted from upcycled cellulose acetate.

Cellulose acetate is the tough, patterned material of high-end eyewear. CloudCaps recovers offcuts of it from La Réserve des Arts in Paris and CNC-machines them into bottle openers built to last.

Because the stock depends on what the fashion industry discards, no two pieces match: tortoiseshell, confetti, marble or a deep solid colour. Every backer opens a one-of-a-kind object.
