Project 01 — Research paper

Can a mushroom replace a plasterboard?

An experimental comparison of mycelium-based composites and the standard BA13 gypsum panel: thermal insulation, fire and water.

Context
Fourth-year innovation project at ESILV, 2025 – 26, in a pair with Paul Simonet
Tools
3D-printed moulds, instrumented XPS hot box (XIAO ESP32-S3, KY-015 sensors), blowtorch and IR pyrometer, moisture meter

Plasterboard is in almost every partition wall, but making it takes a lot of energy. Mycelium binds farm waste into a light, insulating material.

Two formulations were grown: Pleurotus ostreatus (oyster mushroom) on wood chips and Ganoderma lucidum (reishi) on grain. Colonised substrate was crumbled, packed into custom 3D-printed moulds, incubated for a week, unmoulded to grow a protective fungal “skin”, then dried for six hours at 60 °C. Each formulation was tested against BA13 for heat, fire and water.

Verdict. Mycelium is not a like-for-like replacement: plaster stays non-combustible. But on grain it lost only 5.6 % of its mass under the flame and kept its core at 11 % moisture, against 31 % for saturated plaster. On wood chips it lost up to 62 %, and one sample kept burning on its own. Caveat: the mycelium panels were 18 mm thick against 12.5 mm for BA13.

Project 02 — Internship, Paris · Campus Cyber

Reading the weather by radio

Mounting, pairing and maintaining a multi-sensor Ecowitt weather station — then intercepting its own radio frames with a USB dongle.

One of the first assignments of my internship at Campus Cyber: the full physical installation and network configuration of a weather station, from mast alignment to firmware updates.

Then, with an RTL-SDR key — a software-defined radio receiver — we captured, visualised and decoded the raw frames the sensors were broadcasting.

  • GW1100Wi-Fi gateway. Collects every sensor's RF signal; measures indoor temperature, humidity and pressure.
  • WS90 “WittBoy”Seven-in-one array: ultrasonic anemometer, haptic rain gauge, temperature, humidity, UV, light. Solar powered.
  • WH57Lightning detector with a range of up to 40 km.

Project 03 — Internship, French Guiana · Guiana Space Centre

Snake Eye

A mobile, passive forensic radio box designed to map every wireless network around a launch site.

If a launch goes wrong, a spaceport has to be able to show that the anomaly was not caused by an outside action on its radio environment. Snake Eye is the collection kit imagined for that job: one Linux “brain”, three radio modules, no soldering.

Three domains — Bluetooth LE, private Wi-Fi and LoRaWAN — are captured side by side and merged into time-stamped .pcap files.

Mode A

Black box

Boots, launches tshark on every radio interface and records to the SD card, fully offline.

Mode B

Live stream

One SSH command pipes all three captures in real time into Wireshark on the analyst's laptop.

Raspberry Pi OS Lite · rtl8814au (DKMS) · airmon-ng · nRF Sniffer · Python 3 · tshark · OpenSSH

  • Raspberry Pi 5The brain — powers, time-stamps, merges
  • Alfa AWUS1900Wi-Fi 2.4 / 5 GHz, monitor mode
  • nRF52840Bluetooth LE sniffer, follows channel hops
  • RTL-SDR v4LoRaWAN / IoT, 868 MHz antenna

Project 04 — Product design · Kickstarter

CloudCaps

A cloud-shaped bottle opener, CNC-machined from reclaimed eyewear acetate and funded on Kickstarter.

Team
Three classmates, from design and prototyping to the campaign and production
Tools
SolidWorks, CNC machining, reclaimed cellulose acetate, metal opener insert, Kickstarter

Cellulose acetate is what eyeglass frames are made of: good-looking, tough, a little premium. We reclaimed offcuts and unsold sheets, notably from La Réserve des Arts in Paris, to give them a second life. No two sheets match, so every opener has its own pattern.

The cloud shape comes from ergonomics: its rounded lobes sit well in the hand when you open a bottle. Drawn in SolidWorks, it is CNC-machined from the acetate sheets, with a screwed-on metal opener insert. Several prototypes came before a Quickstarter campaign, with photos and a promo video we made ourselves.

Outcome. 135 % funded. The run is being made this autumn, with deliveries planned for December.
